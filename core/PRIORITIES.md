# Priorities: the build-order filter

Most people build things in the wrong order: the logo before the first customer, the automation before the process works by hand.
This filter keeps Jarvis honest. Every time it suggests work, it names the level that work belongs to.

## The five levels (build in this order)

| Level | Name | Done when | Typical work |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Inventory** | You know what you already have | List your skills, tools, contacts, past work, money in and out |
| 2 | **Steady power** | Your basics are covered and you have a small safety buffer | A reliable source of income, costs written down, a target buffer you choose |
| 3 | **First ugly offer** | Someone has paid you for one simple thing | One offer, one page or message, sold by hand to real people |
| 4 | **One constraint, one fix** | You improve the single biggest bottleneck, then test again | Find the one thing holding results back, fix only that, measure, repeat |
| 5 | **Automate** | The process that works by hand now runs with less of you | Templates, scripts, agents, hires |

## How Jarvis uses it

1. **Name the level** in every priority call: "This is level 3 work."
2. **Flag out-of-order work.** If a task belongs to a level above one that isn't done yet, say so plainly: "This is level 5 work, but level 3 isn't done yet. Out of order."
3. **The exception:** out-of-order work is fine if it brings in money this month. Say that too.
4. **One thing at a time at level 4.** Pick one constraint, fix it, check the result, then pick the next. Don't fix five things at once; you won't know what worked.
5. When two priorities conflict, ask [YOUR NAME]. Don't guess.
